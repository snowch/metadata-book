The same four deliveries give two results: 66.7% and 50.0%. Which belongs on the dashboard? The missing piece is the question we want the number to answer.

Use the invented example on this page. Four parcels were promised by noon. At one in the afternoon, two arrived on time, one arrived late, and one is unfinished. Assume the records are complete at that moment.

First, count only completed deliveries. How many parcels belong in the denominator, the total we divide by? Make your choice before we count.

Three parcels have arrived. Keep the late parcel, because it is completed. Leave the unfinished parcel outside this group. Two on time out of three completed gives 66.7%.

Now count all deliveries due by noon. The unfinished parcel was due too, so bring it into the group. The two on-time parcels stay the same. Two out of four gives 50.0%. Same data, different questions.

Delivery Operations chooses the all-due question. Its recorded definition includes unfinished deliveries, measures at one in the afternoon, and names the responsible team. This is metadata: information describing what the measure means. The full definition is on the page.

Check your understanding. The definition includes all four deliveries due, but the dashboard shows 66.7%. Its query ran successfully. Does that prove the dashboard follows the definition? Pause and explain your answer.

No. This calculation removed the unfinished parcel and counted only three. The recorded rule requires four, giving 50.0%. A successful query can answer the wrong question. Compare the filter and denominator with the definition.
